<section className="abstract-band" id="abstract" aria-labelledby="abstract-heading">
  <div className="abstract-copy">
    <h2 id="abstract-heading">Abstract</h2>

Harness optimization can substantially improve LLM agents without modifying model weights, but existing methods primarily optimize response quality. Extending them to joint quality–cost optimization introduces a challenge: applying the deployment preference during intermediate updates can prematurely reject candidates that are valuable for later improvements. We introduce **ParetoSaddler**, which addresses this mismatch by separating **intermediate candidate promotion** from **final deployment selection**. At each iteration, an optimizer updates a parent harness to produce a candidate, and then compares the two over repeated executions and uses Bayesian bootstrap Pareto support to promote candidates showing credible progress in either quality or cost. Promoted candidates receive broader development evaluation and remain available as parents for later optimization steps. We evaluate ParetoSaddler on ClawMark and GAIA2 using domain-disjoint splits. On ClawMark, ParetoSaddler improves quality from **70.86% to 81.35%** while reducing cost from **$1.17 to $1.01** per task; on GAIA2, it improves quality from **56.22% to 62.89%** while reducing cost from **$0.78 to $0.45** per task. The Pareto-selected harnesses also achieve higher quality at lower cost than quality-only harness optimization. Analysis shows that Pareto-aware optimization retains intermediate candidates that would be discarded by deployment preference but can support later improvements, while Bayesian bootstrap screening filters out candidates whose gains are weakly supported across runs.

</section>

    <h2 id="method-heading">Method Overview</h2>
  </div>
  <MethodFigure />
  <ol className="method-steps">
    <li><h3>Execute repeatedly</h3><p>Run the parent and candidate multiple times. Keep quality and token cost paired for each execution to expose persistent failures, unstable behavior, and inefficiency.</p></li>
    <li><h3>Promote credible progress</h3><p>Bayesian bootstrap draws estimate support for improving either quality or cost, with loose safeguards against regression in the other objective.</p></li>
    <li><h3>Retain, optimize, then select</h3><p>Evaluate promoted candidates on the development set. Retain all evaluated candidates, including those outside the current Pareto archive, for later optimization steps.</p></li>
  </ol>
  <aside className="principle">
    <p>Deployment preferences can guide the search.<br /><strong>They do not act as a hard intermediate filter.</strong></p>
  </aside>
  <details className="technical-detail">
    <summary>Inside the statistical promotion rule</summary>
    <PromotionRule />
    <details className="promotion-equations">
      <summary>Definition and experimental settings</summary>
    <div>
      <p>Within each task and harness, draw Dirichlet weights over repeated runs. Apply the same weight to each run’s quality and cost, then average tasks equally. A draw supports promotion if:</p>
      <p className="rule">(ΔQ &gt; 0 and ΔC ≤ ε<sub>C</sub>) or (ΔC &lt; 0 and ΔQ ≥ −ε<sub>Q</sub>)</p>
      <p>ΔQ is the absolute quality change; ΔC is the relative cost change. Promote when the fraction of supporting draws is at least γ. Experiments use <strong>3 runs/task, 10,000 draws, γ = 0.7, ε<sub>Q</sub> = 0.1, and ε<sub>C</sub> = ∞</strong>. These are local safeguards, separate from the final deployment constraint.</p>
    </div>
    </details>
