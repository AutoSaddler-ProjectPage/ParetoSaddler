<section className="abstract-section" id="abstract" aria-labelledby="abstract-heading">
  <div className="reading-width">
    <p className="eyebrow">ABSTRACT</p>
    <h2 id="abstract-heading">Better quality. Lower cost.<br />Room to explore.</h2>

Harness optimization can substantially improve LLM agents without modifying model weights, but existing methods primarily optimize response quality. Extending them to joint quality–cost optimization introduces a challenge: applying the deployment preference during intermediate updates can prematurely reject candidates that are valuable for later improvements.

We introduce **ParetoSaddler**, which separates **intermediate candidate promotion** from **final deployment selection**. At each iteration, an optimizer updates a parent harness to produce a candidate, compares the two over repeated executions, and uses Bayesian bootstrap Pareto support to promote candidates showing credible progress in either quality or cost. Promoted candidates receive broader development evaluation and remain available as parents for later optimization steps.

On domain-disjoint test sets, ParetoSaddler improves quality while reducing cost on both ClawMark and GAIA2. Its Pareto-selected harnesses also achieve higher quality at lower cost than quality-only harness optimization. Analyses show that preserving intermediate trade-offs and accounting for execution uncertainty are both essential to this progress.

    <p className="eyebrow">01 / METHOD</p>
    <h2 id="method-heading">Explore now. Select at deployment.</h2>
    <p>A candidate can be a useful stepping stone before it becomes the harness you want to deploy.</p>
  </div>
  <MethodFigure />
  <ol className="method-steps">
    <li><span className="step-number">01</span><h3>Execute repeatedly</h3><p>Run the parent and candidate multiple times. Keep quality and token cost paired for each execution to expose persistent failures, unstable behavior, and inefficiency.</p></li>
    <li><span className="step-number">02</span><h3>Promote credible progress</h3><p>Bayesian bootstrap draws estimate support for improving either quality or cost, with loose safeguards against regression in the other objective.</p></li>
    <li><span className="step-number">03</span><h3>Retain, refine, then select</h3><p>Evaluate promoted candidates on the development set. Retain all evaluated candidates, including those outside the current Pareto archive, for future refinement.</p></li>
  </ol>
  <aside className="principle">
    <span className="principle-label">THE KEY DISTINCTION</span>
    <p>Deployment preferences can guide the search.<br /><strong>They do not act as a hard intermediate filter.</strong></p>
  </aside>
  <details className="technical-detail">
    <summary>Inside the statistical promotion rule</summary>
    <div>
      <p>Within each task and harness, draw Dirichlet weights over repeated runs. Apply the same weight to each run’s quality and cost, then average tasks equally. A draw supports promotion if:</p>
      <p className="rule">(ΔQ &gt; 0 and ΔC ≤ ε<sub>C</sub>) or (ΔC &lt; 0 and ΔQ ≥ −ε<sub>Q</sub>)</p>
      <p>ΔQ is the absolute quality change; ΔC is the relative cost change. Promote when the fraction of supporting draws is at least γ. Experiments use <strong>3 runs/task, 10,000 draws, γ = 0.7, ε<sub>Q</sub> = 0.1, and ε<sub>C</sub> = ∞</strong>. These are local safeguards, separate from the final deployment constraint.</p>
    </div>
  </details>
